What you train

Kyokushin practice rests on three parts. Every student works on all three as they move up through the grades.

Kihon

Basics

Stances, punches, kicks, blocks and movement, repeated until they hold up under pressure.

Kata

Forms

Set sequences of techniques that teach balance, rhythm, timing and focus.

Kumite

Sparring

Full-contact sparring, built up gradually, where the basics get tested against a real partner.

Bradley Chisholm has trained in Kyokushin karate for over 13 years. He started in New Zealand in 2013 at seven years old. After moving to Australia in 2014 he joined the Ballina Dojo under Shihan Jon Ellis.

He earned his 1st Dan in June 2022 under Hanshi Howard Lipman, and his 2nd Dan on 7 December 2024 under Shihan Rick Cunningham. He competes in kata and full-contact kumite. Results include gold at the Gold Coast Budo Challenge in 2023, silver in 2024, and in 2026 knockdown divisions won at the AMAC Championships in Port Macquarie and the So-Kyokushin National Championships in Sydney.

Brad spent three years instructing at the Ballina Dojo, teaching students of different ages and abilities. In 2025 he moved to Coffs Harbour and started Kyokushin Karate Coffs Harbour. In 2026 he also began training judo, and has reached orange belt (4th kyu).

He teaches karate as more than fighting. It builds focus, confidence, respect and self-discipline, and those carry well beyond the dojo. Classes combine traditional values with an encouraging environment where every student is challenged to improve, whatever their starting ability or experience.

The belt path

MOKKA uses two belt systems. Adults wear solid belts. Juniors wear a belt with a coloured stripe and move up in smaller steps.

Adults

  1. White
  2. Orange
  3. Blue
  4. Yellow
  5. Green
  6. Brown
  7. Black

Black belt is where the dan grades begin, not the end of training.

Juniors

  1. Orange3 levels (10th kyu), then 9th kyu
  2. Blue3 levels (8th kyu), then 7th kyu
  3. Yellow3 levels (6th kyu), then 5th kyu
  4. Green3 levels (4th kyu), then 3rd kyu
  5. Brown3 levels (2nd kyu), then 1st kyu
  6. Junior blackBlack with a white stripe

Solid belt colours are for students from about 15 or 16 years old. The junior black belt is for juniors who have reached a high technical and personal standard but are not yet ready to safely attempt a senior shodan (black belt) grading. Ask your instructor if you have any questions.

Come and train

The best way to find out whether kyokushin suits you is to watch a class or try one.

Before your first class

  • Wear loose, comfortable clothes. A uniform is not needed to start.
  • Arrive a few minutes early and bring water.
  • Tell the instructor about any injuries or health conditions.
  • Expect to hear "Osu". It is the greeting and the answer to almost everything in the dojo.
